SHOT 2025

The Prometheans sponsored four panels at the Society for the History of Technology’s annual meeting on the Belval Campus of the University of Luxembourg, 9–11 October 2025, hosted by the Luxembourg Centre for Contemporary and Digital History. The meeting’s themes were technologies of migration and migrating technologies. The group’s call for papers began from the observation that engineers and technologies have often migrated across geographical and cultural borders. All four panels ran on Friday 10 October.

What emerged

Writing up the panels afterwards for Engineering Studies, the organisers drew out three questions that ran across the fourteen presentations.

  1. Why does it matter that engineers migrated? Engineers carried more than technical skill — they transported political assumptions, institutional norms and forms of authority, reconfiguring labour systems and the relationship between states and experts. They have often held an outsized significance despite their small numbers.
  2. Why does the nation persist as an analytical category? Even where technologies and experts crossed borders, their meaning, legitimacy and consequences were negotiated through national political projects.
  3. What role can engineering history play in changing popular narratives of engineering? Several presenters showed that the most widely circulating engineering histories are misinformed or oversimplified, and that correcting them is urgent.

The full report is published as Ryan Hearty and Ellan Spero, “Report on the SHOT Annual Meeting, ‘Technologies of Migration – Migrating Technologies’, 9–11 October 2025,” Engineering Studies 18, no. 2 (2026): 201–8. doi:10.1080/19378629.2026.2629252
