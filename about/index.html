About

A community built around engineers as subjects of serious historical inquiry, and engineering as a set of significant practices in the modern world — historically grounded, international and interdisciplinary.

The Prometheans run in two venues, and the difference between them is the point. The Special Interest Group gives engineers, engineering work, pedagogy and contemporary practice a visible platform inside the Society for the History of Technology’s annual meeting. The Engineering Studies working group at the Consortium for History of Science, Technology and Medicine is the more informal and more sustained forum: a monthly hour for conversation across disciplinary boundaries and spheres of practice, with low barriers to participation.

Between them they describe an opportunity at the intersection of history, engineering studies and the applied technical fields — a space where historically informed analysis can sharpen how scholars, educators and practitioners alike consider engineers as people, engineering as a set of collective practices, and technology as a force shaping social, political and environmental life.

Much of what the group does is practical. Work in progress gets its first hearing in the working group; panels for the following year’s SHOT meeting are built there over the winter and spring; and what is presented at the meeting comes back to the group afterwards for a debrief.

Taking part

The working group meets on Friday mornings, usually at 11:00 Eastern, through the Consortium. Joining a session requires a Consortium account, which is free, and the group has just over two hundred members. The SHOT sessions are open to everyone registered for the meeting.
