About

The Prometheans foreground engineers and engineering in historical scholarship. The aim is a historically grounded, international and interdisciplinary community focused on engineers as subjects of serious historical inquiry, and on engineering as a set of significant practices in the modern world.

The group runs in two venues. The Prometheans Special Interest Group provides a visible platform within the Society for the History of Technology for foregrounding engineers, engineering work, pedagogy and contemporary practice. The Engineering Studies working group at the Consortium for History of Science, Technology and Medicine offers a more informal and sustained forum for conversation across disciplinary boundaries and spheres of practice. It is a forum for early-stage historical work on engineers and engineering in science, technology and medicine, with low barriers to participation.

The two venues work together. Work in progress is presented in the working group, panels for the following year’s SHOT meeting are developed there over the winter and spring, and the panels are revisited in the working group after the meeting.

The group’s wider interest lies at the intersection of history, engineering studies and the applied technical fields, where historically informed analysis can sharpen how scholars, educators and practitioners consider engineers as people, engineering as a set of collective practices, and technology as a force shaping social, political and environmental life.

Taking part

The working group meets on Friday mornings, usually at 11:00 Eastern, through the Consortium. Joining a session requires a Consortium account, which is free. The group has just over two hundred members. The SHOT sessions are open to everyone registered for the meeting.

Conveners

Ellan F. Spero teaches in the Technology and Policy Program, Institute for Data, Systems and Society, and the Department of Materials Science and Engineering at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and is a co-founder and Professor of the Practice at Station1.

Ryan Hearty teaches at the Center for Leadership Education in the Whiting School of Engineering at Johns Hopkins University.

Convened by Ellan F. Spero (Massachusetts Institute of Technology) and Ryan Hearty (Johns Hopkins University).
